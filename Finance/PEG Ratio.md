The PEG ratio adjusts the [[Price to Earnings Ratio|P/E ratio]] for expected earnings growth, so that a fast-growing company with a high P/E is not automatically labelled expensive.

This note duplicates [[Price Earnings to Growth Ratio (PEG)]], which records the forward-growth convention; the two have not been merged.

## Formula

$$
\text{PEG} = \frac{\text{P/E}}{\text{EPS growth rate (in \%)}}
$$

Growth is entered as a number of percentage points, so 25% growth enters as 25, not 0.25.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** a P/E of 20 and expected EPS growth of 25% a year.

**Step 1: PEG.**

$$
\frac{20}{25} = 0.8
$$

A PEG below 1 is often read as the market price not fully reflecting growth. The author's screen accepts PEG below 2; see [[Stock Screening - Financial Ratios]].

## Interpretation

- A lower PEG suggests the stock may be undervalued relative to its growth; a higher one, overvalued.
- The result is only as good as the growth forecast, and the ratio breaks down for companies with negative or very low growth.
