Cost of goods sold (COGS), also called **cost of sales**, is the direct cost of producing the goods or services sold in the period: the purchase cost of goods resold, or the materials and labour used to make them.[^sec-guide] Service businesses often call it **cost of revenue**. It sits just below revenue on the [[P&L Statement]].

## Formula

For a company that holds inventory:

$$
\text{COGS} = \text{Opening inventory} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Closing inventory}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** opening inventory 200, purchases 900 and closing inventory 250.

**Step 1: COGS.**

$$
200 + 900 - 250 = 850
$$

The company had 1,100 of goods available and 250 remain, so 850 of cost is matched against this period's sales. Revenue minus COGS gives [[Gross Profit]].

## Related Notes

- [[Inventory Turnover]] — uses COGS in the numerator.
- [[Selling, General and Administrative Expense]] — costs not directly tied to production, excluded from COGS.
