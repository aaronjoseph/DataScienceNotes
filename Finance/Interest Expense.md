Interest expense is the cost of borrowed money. On the [[P&L Statement]] it is the interest **accrued** for the period, not necessarily the interest paid in cash during it; cash interest appears in the [[Cash Flow Statement]].[^sec-guide]

## Author's Risk Ratio

The share of operating income consumed by interest shows how much risk the company has taken on:

$$
\text{Interest burden} = \frac{\text{Interest expense}}{\text{Operating income}}
$$

It is the inverse of the [[Interest Coverage Ratio]].

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** interest expense 40 and operating income (EBIT) 220.

**Step 1: ratio.**

$$
\frac{40}{220} \approx 18.2\%
$$

About 18% of operating profit goes to lenders before tax and shareholders. The higher this share, the less room the company has if profits fall or rates rise.
