Profitability ratios measure how well a company turns sales, assets and capital into profit. Profits fund expansion and dividends, so these ratios are central to judging the business and the competitiveness of its management.

## Major Ratios

1. [[EBITDA Margin]] — operating profit margin; the author also tracks EBITDA growth.
2. [[Profit After Tax (PAT)]] margin — with PAT growth measured as a compound annual growth rate (CAGR).
3. [[Return on Equity (ROE)]]
4. [[Return on Asset|Return on Assets (ROA)]]
5. [[Return on Capital Employed (RoCE)]]

## Growth as a CAGR

$$
\text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{\text{Ending value}}{\text{Beginning value}}\right)^{1/n} - 1
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** PAT of 600 three years ago and 975 now.

**Step 1: growth multiple.**

$$
\frac{975}{600} = 1.625
$$

**Step 2: three-year CAGR.**

$$
1.625^{1/3} - 1 \approx 17.6\%
$$

Profit grew about 17.6% a year, above the author's 15% target in the [[Stock Selection Checklist]].
