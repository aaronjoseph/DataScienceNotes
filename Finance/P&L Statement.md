The profit and loss (P&L) statement, also called the income statement, statement of operations or statement of earnings, shows how much revenue a company earned over a period and the costs and expenses of earning it. Unlike the [[Balance Sheet]], which is a snapshot, it covers a period of time. The bottom line is net profit or loss.[^sec-guide]

Profit is not cash: revenue and expenses are recorded when earned or incurred, not when cash moves. The [[Cash Flow Statement]] shows the cash.

## Structure

The SEC describes the income statement as a set of stairs, deducting one kind of cost at each step:[^sec-guide]

1. [[Revenue]] (sales), less returns and allowances.
2. Less [[Cost of Goods Sold]] = [[Gross Profit]].
3. Less [[Operating Expense|operating expenses]] ([[Selling, General and Administrative Expense|SG&A]], [[R&D Costs|R&D]], [[Amortization & Depreciation|depreciation and amortisation]]) = [[Operating Income]].
4. Less [[Interest Expense|interest]], plus or minus other items such as [[Gain (Or Loss) On Sale of Assets and Others|gains or losses on asset sales]] = [[Profit Before Tax (PBT)]].
5. Less tax = [[Profit After Tax (PAT)]], also called net income.

## Worked Example

All figures in millions. The earlier version's table did not add up (its operating items left 3,200 before tax, not the 1,500 shown) and omitted depreciation. The figures below keep its revenue, COGS, SG&A, R&D, interest, PBT, tax and PAT, and add illustrative depreciation and a small loss on asset sales so that each step is consistent.

| Line item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue | 10,000 |
| Cost of goods sold | (3,000) |
| **Gross profit** | 7,000 |
| SG&A | (2,100) |
| R&D | (1,000) |
| Depreciation and amortisation | (1,690) |
| **Operating income** | 2,210 |
| Interest expense | (700) |
| Loss on sale of assets | (10) |
| **Profit before tax** | 1,500 |
| Tax expense | (525) |
| **Profit after tax** | 975 |

**Step 1: gross profit.**

$$
10{,}000 - 3{,}000 = 7{,}000
$$

**Step 2: operating income.**

$$
7{,}000 - 2{,}100 - 1{,}000 - 1{,}690 = 2{,}210
$$

**Step 3: profit before tax.**

$$
2{,}210 - 700 - 10 = 1{,}500
$$

**Step 4: profit after tax.**

$$
1{,}500 - 525 = 975
$$

Of 10,000 of sales, 975 (9.75%) reaches shareholders. The biggest deductions are production costs and SG&A; interest takes about a third of operating income, which is worth checking with the [[Interest Coverage Ratio]].
