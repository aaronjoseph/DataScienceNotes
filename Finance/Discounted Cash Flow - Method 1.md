The intrinsic value of a stock, bond or business is the value of the cash it is expected to produce over its remaining life, discounted at an appropriate rate and added together. Two questions drive it:

1. How much cash will the business make in the future?
2. What is that future cash worth to you today?

See [[Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)]] for the underlying time-value formulas.

## Step 1: Owner Earnings or Free Cash Flow

Owner earnings is the cash the business produces while staying at its current size:

$$
\text{Owner earnings} = \text{Operating cash flow} - \text{Maintenance CapEx}
$$

- **Operating cash flow** is cash generated by the normal business, from the [[Cash Flow Statement]].
- **Maintenance CapEx** is the [[Capital Expenditure]] needed to keep operating as today. Most companies do not report it separately.

When maintenance CapEx is unknown, use [[Free Cash Flow]], which deducts all CapEx and so is more conservative:

$$
\text{Free cash flow} = \text{Operating cash flow} - \text{Total CapEx}
$$

## Step 2: Project and Discount

Project free cash flow for 10 years, discount each year at the required return $r$, and add a terminal value for the years after that.

$$
\text{Intrinsic value} = \sum_{t=1}^{10} \frac{\text{FCF}_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{\text{TV}_{10}}{(1 + r)^{10}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** free cash flow of 100 in year 1, growing 10% a year; a 15% required return; and a terminal value of 10 times the year-10 cash flow.

| Year | Free cash flow | Present value at 15% |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100.0 | 87.0 |
| 2 | 110.0 | 83.2 |
| 3 | 121.0 | 79.6 |
| 4 | 133.1 | 76.1 |
| 5 | 146.4 | 72.8 |
| 6 | 161.1 | 69.6 |
| 7 | 177.2 | 66.6 |
| 8 | 194.9 | 63.7 |
| 9 | 214.4 | 60.9 |
| 10 | 235.8 | 58.3 |

**Step 1: present value of year 1.**

$$
\frac{100}{1.15} = 87.0
$$

**Step 2: sum of the ten present values.**

$$
87.0 + 83.2 + \dots + 58.3 = 717.7
$$

**Step 3: terminal value and its present value.**

$$
10 \times 235.8 = 2{,}357.9, \qquad \frac{2{,}357.9}{1.15^{10}} = 582.8
$$

**Step 4: intrinsic value.**

$$
717.7 + 582.8 = 1{,}300.6
$$

The business is worth about 1,300 units today. The earlier version of this table reported 2,000, because it multiplied every year's cash flow by 0.85 instead of dividing by $1.15^t$, so later years were barely discounted. Recalculated in Python.

## Step 3: Margin of Safety

> The function of the margin of safety is, in essence, that of rendering unnecessary an accurate estimate of the future.

This line is commonly attributed to Benjamin Graham's *The Intelligent Investor*; the source was not checked here. A margin of safety is needed because valuation is imprecise, the future is unpredictable, and investors make mistakes.

**Author's rule:** buy only below the intrinsic value minus a 30–50% discount; 30% for a seasoned investor and 50% for a new one. For this example:

$$
1{,}300.6 \times 0.7 = 910.4, \qquad 1{,}300.6 \times 0.5 = 650.3
$$
