The financial leverage ratio, also called the **equity multiplier**, shows how many units of assets each unit of shareholders' equity supports. The rest of the assets is funded by liabilities. It is one of the [[Leverage Ratios]] and the third factor in the DuPont breakdown of [[Return on Equity (ROE)]].

## Formula

$$
\text{Financial leverage} = \frac{\text{Average total assets}}{\text{Average total equity}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** average total assets 1,200 and average equity 600.

**Step 1: ratio.**

$$
\frac{1{,}200}{600} = 2
$$

Each unit of equity supports two units of assets, so half the assets are funded by liabilities. The higher the ratio, the higher the leverage: it magnifies ROE in good years and losses in bad ones.
