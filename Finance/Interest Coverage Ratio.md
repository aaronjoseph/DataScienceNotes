The interest coverage ratio shows how many times a company's operating profit covers its interest bill. It is one of the [[Leverage Ratios]]: the higher the ratio, the more easily the company can service its debt.

The earlier version listed "debt service coverage ratio" as a synonym. That is a different measure: debt service coverage also includes principal repayments, not just interest.

## Formula

$$
\text{Interest coverage} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest expense}}
$$

EBIT is [[EBITDA Margin|EBITDA]] minus depreciation and amortisation.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** EBITDA 280, D&A 60 and interest expense 40.

**Step 1: EBIT.**

$$
280 - 60 = 220
$$

**Step 2: coverage.**

$$
\frac{220}{40} = 5.5
$$

Operating profit covers interest 5.5 times, so profit could fall by more than 80% before it no longer covered interest. A ratio near or below 1 means the company cannot pay its interest from operating profit.
