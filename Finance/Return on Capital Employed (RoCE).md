Return on capital employed (ROCE) measures operating profit relative to all the capital used in the business, both equity and debt, long-term and short-term. Unlike [[Return on Equity (ROE)]], it cannot be flattered by borrowing more. It is one of the [[Profitability Ratios]].

## Formula

$$
\text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Capital employed}}
$$

Capital employed is commonly measured as equity plus debt, or as total assets minus current liabilities. EBIT is profit before interest and tax.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** EBIT 2,210, equity 6,000 and total debt 3,000.

**Step 1: capital employed.**

$$
6{,}000 + 3{,}000 = 9{,}000
$$

**Step 2: ROCE.**

$$
\frac{2{,}210}{9{,}000} \approx 24.6\%
$$

The business earns about 25% on all the capital invested in it, well above the author's 15% screen in [[Stock Screening - Financial Ratios]]. A ROCE above the company's cost of capital means growth creates value.
