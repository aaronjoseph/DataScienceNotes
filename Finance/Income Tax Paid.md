Income tax is the tax charged on a company's taxable profit. Two figures are easy to confuse:

- **Tax expense** on the [[P&L Statement]] is the tax attributable to the period's profit, including deferred tax.
- **Income tax paid** in the [[Cash Flow Statement]] is the cash actually paid in the period.

## Effective Tax Rate

$$
\text{Effective tax rate} = \frac{\text{Tax expense}}{\text{Profit before tax}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** profit before tax of 1,500 and tax expense of 525.

**Step 1: effective rate.**

$$
\frac{525}{1{,}500} = 35\%
$$

Compare this with the statutory rate in the company's main jurisdiction.

## Author's Red-Flag Check

Some companies report more profit than they really made. One check is to compare the tax charge with [[Profit Before Tax (PBT)]]: if the implied rate is far below what the reported profit should attract, the profit may be overstated.

Treat this as a prompt to investigate, not as proof. Legitimate reasons for a low rate include tax credits, losses carried forward, profits in lower-tax countries, and timing differences recorded as deferred tax (see [[Non-Current Liabilities]]).
