Leverage ratios, also called solvency or gearing ratios, measure how much a company relies on debt and whether it can meet its long-term obligations. A company must pay its bills and service its debt to keep operating, so these ratios speak to its long-term sustainability.

## Major Ratios

1. [[Interest Coverage Ratio]] — how many times operating profit covers interest.
2. [[Debt to Equity Ratio]] — debt relative to shareholders' funds.
3. [[Debt to Asset Ratio]] — the share of assets financed by debt.
4. [[Financial Leverage Ratio]] — assets supported by each unit of equity.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** total assets 1,200, equity 600, debt 300, EBIT 220 and interest 40.

**Step 1: debt to equity.**

$$
\frac{300}{600} = 0.5
$$

**Step 2: debt to assets.**

$$
\frac{300}{1{,}200} = 0.25
$$

**Step 3: financial leverage.**

$$
\frac{1{,}200}{600} = 2
$$

**Step 4: interest coverage.**

$$
\frac{220}{40} = 5.5
$$

The balance-sheet ratios show moderate debt, and the coverage ratio shows profits comfortably cover the interest. Read them together: low debt with weak coverage, or high debt with strong coverage, tell different stories.
