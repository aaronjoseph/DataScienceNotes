Inventory turnover shows how many times a company sells through its average inventory in a period. A product in high demand leaves the warehouse quickly, which pushes the ratio up. It is one of the [[Operating Ratios]].

## Formula

$$
\text{Inventory turnover} = \frac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Average inventory}}
$$

Average inventory is the mean of the opening and closing balances.[^sec-guide] See [[Cost of Goods Sold]].

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** COGS 850, opening inventory 200 and closing inventory 250.

**Step 1: average inventory.**

$$
\frac{200 + 250}{2} = 225
$$

**Step 2: turnover.**

$$
\frac{850}{225} \approx 3.78
$$

The company turned over its inventory about 3.8 times in the year, or every 96.6 days; see [[Inventory Number of Days]]. A turnover of 2 means inventory turned over twice in the period.[^sec-guide]

## Interpretation

A falling turnover can mean weakening demand or a build-up of unsold stock. A very high turnover can also mean stock-outs and lost sales, so compare with peers.
