Revenue (sales) is the top line of the [[P&L Statement]]: the money a company earns from selling its goods and services during the period. Gross revenue less returns, allowances and discounts gives **net revenue**.[^sec-guide] Revenue is recognised when earned, which is not always when the cash arrives; see [[Account Receivables & Account Payable]].

## From Revenue to Earnings

$$
\text{Earnings} = \text{Revenue} - \text{Total expenses}
$$

A good company can grow earnings by increasing revenue over time, or by cutting expenses while holding revenue steady; growth in revenue is usually more durable.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** gross sales of 10,400, with returns and allowances of 400; total expenses of 9,025.

**Step 1: net revenue.**

$$
10{,}400 - 400 = 10{,}000
$$

**Step 2: earnings.**

$$
10{,}000 - 9{,}025 = 975
$$

Only about 10% of revenue survives as profit here, so a 10% rise in revenue with costs held flat would roughly double earnings.
