The receivables turnover ratio shows how many times in a period the company collects its average receivables balance. It is one of the [[Operating Ratios]].

## Formula

$$
\text{Receivables turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average receivables}}
$$

Average receivables is usually the mean of the opening and closing balances. Credit sales are the more precise numerator when cash sales are material, but they are rarely disclosed.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** revenue of 1,200, opening receivables of 100 and closing receivables of 140.

**Step 1: average receivables.**

$$
\frac{100 + 140}{2} = 120
$$

**Step 2: turnover.**

$$
\frac{1{,}200}{120} = 10
$$

The company collected its average receivables about 10 times in the year, or roughly every 36.5 days; see [[Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)]].

## Interpretation

A higher ratio means cash is collected more quickly. Compare within an industry, because credit terms differ widely, and watch the trend: a falling ratio can mean looser credit or collection problems. See [[Account Receivables & Account Payable]].
