Research and development (R&D) costs are what a company spends to develop, design and improve its products, services, technologies or processes, including any intellectual property created. They are an [[Operating Expense]] on the [[P&L Statement]].[^sec-guide]

## R&D Intensity

$$
\text{R\&D intensity} = \frac{\text{R\&D expense}}{\text{Revenue}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** R&D of 1,000 and revenue of 10,000.

**Step 1: intensity.**

$$
\frac{1{,}000}{10{,}000} = 10\%
$$

The company spends 10% of sales on R&D. That is modest for a pharmaceutical or semiconductor company and very high for a retailer, so compare within the industry.

## Author's View on High R&D Costs

- Companies that depend on heavy R&D to stay competitive can put their long-term economics at risk: the spending must continue, and it does not always produce winners.
- New products from R&D also need new sales programmes, which raises [[Selling, General and Administrative Expense|SG&A]] and eats further into [[Gross Profit]].
