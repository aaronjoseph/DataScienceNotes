EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation: a measure of profit from core operations before financing costs, taxes and non-cash charges. The **EBITDA margin** expresses it as a share of operating revenue and is often read as a gauge of operating efficiency. It is one of the [[Profitability Ratios]].

## Formulas

This note follows a common Indian-market convention that strips other income out of revenue.

**Operating revenue.**

$$
\text{Operating revenue} = \text{Total revenue} - \text{Other income}
$$

**Operating expense.**

$$
\text{Operating expense} = \text{Total expense} - \text{Finance cost} - \text{D\&A}
$$

**EBITDA.**

$$
\text{EBITDA} = \text{Operating revenue} - \text{Operating expense}
$$

**EBITDA margin.**

$$
\text{EBITDA margin} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Operating revenue}}
$$

Subtracting depreciation and amortisation gives EBIT, which the [[Interest Coverage Ratio]] uses:

$$
\text{EBIT} = \text{EBITDA} - \text{D\&A}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** total revenue 1,050 including other income of 50; total expenses 820 including finance cost 40 and D&A 60.

**Step 1: operating revenue.**

$$
1{,}050 - 50 = 1{,}000
$$

**Step 2: operating expense.**

$$
820 - 40 - 60 = 720
$$

**Step 3: EBITDA and margin.**

$$
1{,}000 - 720 = 280, \qquad \frac{280}{1{,}000} = 28\%
$$

**Step 4: EBIT.**

$$
280 - 60 = 220
$$

The core business keeps 28 of every 100 of sales before interest, tax and D&A. Compare the margin with peers and over time; the author also tracks EBITDA growth.

## Pitfalls

- EBITDA ignores the cost of replacing assets. For capital-heavy businesses, depreciation is a real economic cost; check [[Capital Expenditure]] and [[Free Cash Flow]].
- Definitions vary between companies and data providers (for example, whether other income or one-off items are excluded), so recompute it from the statements before comparing.
