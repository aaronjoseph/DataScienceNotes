Total asset turnover measures how much revenue a company generates from its assets, both fixed and current. It is one of the [[Operating Ratios]] and a factor of the DuPont breakdown in [[Return on Equity (ROE)]].

## Formula

$$
\text{Total asset turnover} = \frac{\text{Operating revenue}}{\text{Average total assets}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** revenue 10,000 and total assets of 11,500 at the start of the year and 12,500 at the end.

**Step 1: average total assets.**

$$
\frac{11{,}500 + 12{,}500}{2} = 12{,}000
$$

**Step 2: turnover.**

$$
\frac{10{,}000}{12{,}000} \approx 0.83
$$

Each unit of assets produced 0.83 units of sales. A ratio higher than the company's history and its competitors' means it is using its assets well to generate sales. Asset-heavy industries such as utilities naturally have low turnover, so compare within an industry.
