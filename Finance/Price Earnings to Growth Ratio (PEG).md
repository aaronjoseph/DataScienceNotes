The price/earnings-to-growth (PEG) ratio divides the [[Price to Earnings Ratio|P/E ratio]] by the growth rate of earnings over a specified period. This note duplicates [[PEG Ratio]]; the two have not been merged.

## Formula

$$
\text{PEG} = \frac{\text{Price} / \text{EPS}}{\text{EPS growth (in \%)}}
$$

## Forward Convention

The screening site Tickertape, as the author recorded, uses one-year forward EPS growth:

$$
\text{PEG} = \frac{\text{P/E}}{\text{1-year forward EPS growth}}
$$

Forward growth is a forecast, so the PEG changes with analysts' estimates. Trailing growth uses history and is less forward-looking. Always check which one a data source uses.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** price 300, EPS 15, forecast EPS growth of 12% for next year.

**Step 1: P/E.**

$$
\frac{300}{15} = 20
$$

**Step 2: forward PEG.**

$$
\frac{20}{12} \approx 1.67
$$

A PEG of 1.67 passes the author's screen of below 2, but it is above 1, so growth only partly justifies the P/E.
