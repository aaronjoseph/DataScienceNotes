Valuation ratios compare a company's share price with its sales, profits or book value to judge whether the stock is cheap or expensive. In short, they compare the cost of the security with what owning it gives you.

## Major Ratios

1. [[Price to Sales|Price to Sales (P/S)]]
2. [[Price to Book Value|Price to Book Value (P/BV)]]
3. [[Price to Earnings Ratio|Price to Earnings (P/E)]]

Related measures: [[PEG Ratio]], [[EV by EBITDA|EV/EBITDA]], [[Shiller PE Ratio]] and [[Divident Yield|dividend yield]].

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** share price 50, EPS 2.5, sales per share 10 and book value per share 25.

**Step 1: P/E.**

$$
\frac{50}{2.5} = 20
$$

**Step 2: P/S.**

$$
\frac{50}{10} = 5
$$

**Step 3: P/BV.**

$$
\frac{50}{25} = 2
$$

No single ratio is "right". Compare each with the company's own history and its peers, and check that a low multiple is not a [[Value Trap]].
