Profit after tax (PAT), also called net income, net profit or net income after taxes, is the bottom line of the [[P&L Statement]]: profit after all expenses, interest and taxes.[^sec-guide]

The earlier version called it the net operating profit after tax. That is a different measure (NOPAT), which applies tax to operating profit before interest.

## Formulas

**PAT.**

$$
\text{PAT} = \text{PBT} - \text{Tax expense}
$$

**PAT margin.**

$$
\text{PAT margin} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{Total revenue}}
$$

See [[Profit Before Tax (PBT)]].

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** PBT 1,500, tax expense 525 and revenue 10,000.

**Step 1: PAT.**

$$
1{,}500 - 525 = 975
$$

**Step 2: PAT margin.**

$$
\frac{975}{10{,}000} = 9.75\%
$$

The company keeps about 10 of every 100 of sales as profit for shareholders. Companies with consistently high PAT margins tend to be more competitive. The author also tracks PAT growth as a compound annual growth rate; see [[Profitability Ratios]].
