Capital expenditure (CapEx) is the cash a company spends on buying or upgrading long-term assets such as property, plant and equipment, to maintain or grow the business. It appears as an outflow in the investing section of the [[Cash Flow Statement]], and the assets are then depreciated over their useful lives; see [[Amortization & Depreciation]].

## Maintenance versus Growth CapEx

- **Maintenance CapEx** keeps the existing business running at its current capacity.
- **Growth CapEx** expands capacity or enters new areas.

Companies rarely report the split, which matters when estimating owner earnings in [[Discounted Cash Flow - Method 1]].

## CapEx and Free Cash Flow

$$
\text{Free cash flow} = \text{Cash from operations} - \text{CapEx}
$$

See [[Free Cash Flow]].

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** net earnings of 400 and CapEx of 150.

**Step 1: CapEx as a share of earnings.**

$$
\frac{150}{400} = 37.5\%
$$

The company reinvests just over a third of its earnings in fixed assets, which passes the author's 50% threshold below.
