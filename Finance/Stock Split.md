A stock split divides each existing share into several new shares, often to make the price per share more accessible and improve liquidity. The number of [[Shares Outstanding|shares outstanding]] rises by the split ratio and the price per share falls proportionately, so the total value of the holding is unchanged and existing shareholders are not diluted.[^split] Common ratios are 2-for-1 and 3-for-1: two or three new shares for each share held. Per-share figures such as [[Earnings Per Share|EPS]] and the dividend per share fall in the same proportion.[^split]

## Formulas

$$
\text{New shares} = \text{Old shares} \times \text{Split ratio}
$$

$$
\text{New price} = \frac{\text{Old price}}{\text{Split ratio}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** 100 shares at $100 and a 2-for-1 split (the Investor.gov example).[^split]

**Step 1: new share count.**

$$
100 \times 2 = 200
$$

**Step 2: new price.**

$$
\frac{100}{2} = 50
$$

**Step 3: value before and after.**

$$
100 \times 100 = 200 \times 50 = 10{,}000
$$

The investor owns twice as many shares, each worth half as much; nothing of value has been added or lost.
