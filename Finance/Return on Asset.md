Return on assets (ROA) measures how much profit a company earns on the assets it uses. It reflects management's ability to avoid tying up capital in unproductive assets: the higher the ROA, the better. It is one of the [[Profitability Ratios]].

## Formulas

**Author's version.** Adds back after-tax interest, so that the return does not depend on how the assets are financed:

$$
\text{ROA} = \frac{\text{Net income} + \text{Interest} \times (1 - \text{Tax rate})}{\text{Average total assets}}
$$

**Simple version**, used for example in the [[Piotroski Score]]:

$$
\text{ROA}_{\text{simple}} = \frac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Total assets}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** net income 975, interest expense 700, a 35% tax rate and average total assets of 12,000.

**Step 1: after-tax interest.**

$$
700 \times (1 - 0.35) = 455
$$

**Step 2: ROA.**

$$
\frac{975 + 455}{12{,}000} \approx 11.9\%
$$

**Step 3: simple ROA for comparison.**

$$
\frac{975}{12{,}000} \approx 8.1\%
$$

The assets earn about 11.9% before financing costs, but only 8.1% reaches shareholders after interest. Use one definition consistently when comparing companies.
