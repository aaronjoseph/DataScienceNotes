Free cash flow (FCF) is the cash a company generates after paying for its operations and for the capital spending needed to maintain and grow its asset base. Unlike net income, it excludes non-cash charges such as depreciation, and it includes spending on equipment and changes in working capital.

## Formula

$$
\text{FCF} = \text{Cash from operating activities} - \text{Capital expenditure}
$$

Both inputs come from the [[Cash Flow Statement]]. Valuation work sometimes distinguishes free cash flow to the firm (available to lenders and shareholders) from free cash flow to equity (after debt payments).

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** cash from operations 115 and [[Capital Expenditure]] of 50, with net income of 100.

**Step 1: free cash flow.**

$$
115 - 50 = 65
$$

The company earned 100 of profit but only 65 of cash was free for dividends, buybacks, debt repayment or acquisitions. A persistent gap like this is normal for a growing, capital-intensive business, but a company whose FCF is consistently far below its profits deserves a closer look.
