The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio compares a company's share price with its [[Earnings Per Share]]: how much investors pay for each unit of annual profit.[^sec-guide] It is one of the [[Valuation Ratios]].

## Formula

$$
\text{P/E} = \frac{\text{Price per share}}{\text{Earnings per share}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** a share price of 20 and EPS of 2.

**Step 1: P/E.**

$$
\frac{20}{2} = 10
$$

The stock sells at 10 times its earnings.[^sec-guide] Likewise, a P/E of 30 means paying 30 units of currency for 1 unit of annual profit.

## Interpretation

- A high P/E can mean the market expects strong growth, or that the stock is expensive; see [[PEG Ratio]] for a growth adjustment.
- Trailing P/E uses past earnings and forward P/E uses forecasts; a stock that looks cheap on trailing earnings can be a [[Value Trap]].
- Cyclically depressed or inflated earnings distort the ratio; the [[Shiller PE Ratio]] averages earnings over ten years to smooth this.
- The author's screen: current P/E below 1.5 times the 5-year average P/E; see [[Stock Screening - Financial Ratios]].
