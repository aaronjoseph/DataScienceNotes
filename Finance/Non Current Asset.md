Non-current (long-term) assets are assets whose economic benefit is expected to last more than a year. The company does not expect to convert them to cash within that time.[^sec-guide]

## Types

- **Fixed assets** are used to run the business and are not held for sale, such as land, buildings, plant and machinery, vehicles and office furniture.[^sec-guide] They cannot easily be turned into cash.
- **Tangible assets** have a physical form: plant and machinery, vehicles, buildings, fixtures.
- **Intangible assets** have economic value but no physical form: patents, copyrights, trademarks and designs. The earlier version counted them as fixed assets; they also benefit the company for many years, but balance sheets usually report them on a separate line.

## Gross Block and Net Block

When a company acquires a fixed asset, it records the cost as the **gross block**. Depreciation accumulates over the asset's life, and what remains is the **net block**; see [[Amortization & Depreciation]].

$$
\text{Net block} = \text{Gross block} - \text{Accumulated depreciation}
$$

## Components

- **Property, plant and equipment (PP&E).** Carried at original cost less accumulated depreciation.
  - **Author's heuristic, competitive advantage:** the company does not need to upgrade its plant and equipment often.
  - **Author's heuristic, competitive disadvantage:** it must keep upgrading to keep up with competitors.
- **Goodwill.** Arises when a company buys another for more than the fair value of its net assets.
- **Other intangible assets**, such as patents and brands.
- **Long-term investments**, such as stocks, bonds and real estate held for more than a year.
- **Other long-term assets** that do not fit elsewhere, such as prepaid expenses covering more than a year and tax recoveries due in later years.

## Goodwill Formula

With purchase price $P$ and the fair values of the target's assets $A$ and liabilities $L$:

$$
\text{Goodwill} = P - (A - L)
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** a gross block of 5,000 with accumulated depreciation of 1,800; and an acquisition for 1,000 of a company whose assets are worth 900 and liabilities 300.

**Step 1: net block.**

$$
5{,}000 - 1{,}800 = 3{,}200
$$

**Step 2: goodwill.**

$$
1{,}000 - (900 - 300) = 400
$$

The acquirer paid 400 more than the target's net assets were worth; that premium is recorded as goodwill, for expected synergies, brand or customer relationships.
