When a company sells an asset other than inventory, such as a building or a subsidiary, the profit or loss is recorded as a **gain (or loss) on sale of assets**. The same line can hold other income outside the normal course of business, such as licensing agreements or sales of patents.

## Formula

$$
\text{Gain or loss} = \text{Sale proceeds} - \text{Carrying amount}
$$

The carrying amount is the asset's value on the books: its cost less accumulated depreciation.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** a machine bought for 10,000, with accumulated depreciation of 3,600, sold for 7,000.

**Step 1: carrying amount.**

$$
10{,}000 - 3{,}600 = 6{,}400
$$

**Step 2: gain.**

$$
7{,}000 - 6{,}400 = 600
$$

The 600 gain raises profit this year, but it does not recur. Strip such one-off items out when judging the underlying earnings trend, and note that the full 7,000 of cash appears under investing activities in the [[Cash Flow Statement]], not in operating cash flow.
