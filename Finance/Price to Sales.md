The price-to-sales (P/S) ratio values a company on its revenue instead of its earnings. It is useful for companies with little or no profit, such as young or cyclical businesses at a low point. It is one of the [[Valuation Ratios]].

## Formulas

**Sales per share.**

$$
\text{Sales per share} = \frac{\text{Total revenue}}{\text{Number of shares}}
$$

**P/S ratio.**

$$
\text{P/S} = \frac{\text{Share price}}{\text{Sales per share}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** revenue 1,000, 100 shares and a share price of 50.

**Step 1: sales per share.**

$$
\frac{1{,}000}{100} = 10
$$

**Step 2: P/S.**

$$
\frac{50}{10} = 5
$$

Investors pay 5 units for every unit of annual sales. The higher the P/S, the higher the valuation. Because revenue says nothing about profitability, compare P/S only between companies with similar margins.
