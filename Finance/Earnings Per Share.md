Earnings per share (EPS) measures profit per ordinary share: how much each share would receive if the company paid out all of its net income for the period.[^sec-guide] It is reported on the [[P&L Statement]].

## Formula

$$
\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Net income} - \text{Preferred dividends}}{\text{Weighted average common shares outstanding}}
$$

The SEC's simple version divides net income by shares outstanding.[^sec-guide] Without preferred shares, this is [[Profit After Tax (PAT)]] divided by the number of shares. Companies also report **diluted EPS**, which counts shares that options and convertibles could add. [[Shares Outstanding|Treasury shares]] are excluded.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** net income 500, preferred dividends 50 and a weighted average of 100 common shares.

**Step 1: earnings available to common shareholders.**

$$
500 - 50 = 450
$$

**Step 2: EPS.**

$$
\frac{450}{100} = 4.5
$$

Each common share earned 4.5 in the period. The earlier version described EPS as earnings "per face value"; it is per share, regardless of the share's face value.

## Interpretation

- Growing EPS indicates growing profitability per share, but compare its growth with net profit growth: if EPS grows more slowly, the company is diluting shareholders by issuing shares (author's checklist item; see [[Stock Selection Checklist]]).
- Buybacks raise EPS without any change in profit.
- EPS is the denominator of the [[Price to Earnings Ratio]].
