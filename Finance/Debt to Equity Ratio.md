The debt-to-equity ratio compares how much of the company is funded by lenders with how much is funded by shareholders. Both inputs come from the [[Balance Sheet]]. It is one of the [[Leverage Ratios]].

## Formula

This note uses interest-bearing debt:

$$
\text{Debt to equity} = \frac{\text{Long-term borrowings} + \text{Short-term borrowings}}{\text{Shareholders' equity}}
$$

The SEC's beginners' guide defines it more broadly, with **total liabilities** in the numerator.[^sec-guide] The two versions can differ a lot for companies with large payables or provisions, so state which one you use.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** long-term borrowings 200, short-term borrowings 100 and equity 600.

**Step 1: total debt.**

$$
200 + 100 = 300
$$

**Step 2: ratio.**

$$
\frac{300}{600} = 0.5
$$

The company has 0.5 of debt for every 1 of equity. That passes the author's screen of below 0.5 only narrowly; see [[Stock Screening - Financial Ratios]]. Acceptable levels vary by industry: utilities and banks run far higher ratios than software companies.[^sec-guide]
