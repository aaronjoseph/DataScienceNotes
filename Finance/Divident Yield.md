The dividend yield shows how much a company pays out in dividends each year relative to its share price, expressed as a percentage.

## Formula

$$
\text{Dividend yield} = \frac{\text{Annual dividend per share}}{\text{Price per share}}
$$

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** an annual dividend of 5 per share and a share price of 200.

**Step 1: yield.**

$$
\frac{5}{200} = 2.5\%
$$

An investor buying at 200 receives 2.5% a year in dividends, before any change in the share price.

## Pitfalls

- A falling share price raises the yield, so a very high yield can signal that the market expects a dividend cut; see [[Value Trap]].
- Many companies return cash through buybacks instead of dividends, which the dividend yield ignores.
- A [[Stock Split]] cuts the dividend per share and the price in the same proportion, leaving the yield unchanged.
