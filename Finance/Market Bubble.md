A market bubble is a period when asset prices rise far above what fundamentals justify, driven by optimism and speculation, and later fall sharply. This note collects warning signs; it is not a forecasting method.

## Warning Signs

- **Significant and rapid price growth** that looks unsustainable or unprecedented.
- **Excessive optimism** and a lack of caution ("lack of paranoia").
- **Unreasonable confidence** and a suspension of common sense.
- **Market manipulation.**
- **Loose monetary conditions**, for example [[Quantitative Easing]], which can push investors into riskier assets.

## Possible Triggers of a Sharp Fall

The earlier version listed these under signs, but they are shocks that can end a boom rather than evidence of a bubble:

- Trade conflicts and tariffs.
- Wars between nations.
- Geopolitical terror, such as the 9/11 attacks.
- Political assassinations.

## Ray Dalio's Bubble Gauge

The linked article lists these indicators; the list has not been re-checked against the article in this revision.[^dalio]

- High prices relative to traditional measures of value, such as the present value of cash flows; see [[Shiller PE Ratio]].
- Unsustainable conditions, such as extrapolating past revenue and earnings growth late in the cycle.
- Many new and naïve buyers.
- Broad bullish sentiment.
- A high share of purchases financed by debt.
- Many forward and speculative purchases made to bet on price gains.
