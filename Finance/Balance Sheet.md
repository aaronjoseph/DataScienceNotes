The balance sheet shows what a company owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities) and the owners' residual interest (shareholders' equity) **at a single point in time**, the end of the reporting period.[^sec-guide] It is a snapshot: it does not show the flows during the period, which appear in the [[P&L Statement]] and [[Cash Flow Statement]].

The balances are cumulative. Equity, for example, includes all earnings retained since the company was founded, which is what the earlier version of this note meant by "flow basis".

## The Accounting Equation

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Shareholders' equity}
$$

The two sides must always balance.[^sec-guide] The three sections are:

- [[Assets - Balance Sheet]] — current and non-current assets.
- [[Liabilities - Balance Sheet]] — [[Current Liability|current]] and [[Non-Current Liabilities|non-current]] obligations.
- [[Shareholder Equity]] — share capital, reserves and retained earnings.

The earlier version stated "Total Liability = Shareholders' Funds + Non-Current Liabilities + Current Liabilities". That sum is the **total of the liabilities-and-equity side**, which equals total assets, not total liabilities.

## Liabilities

Liabilities are obligations to others, such as bank loans, amounts owed to suppliers, wages, taxes and obligations to deliver goods or services in the future.[^sec-guide] Companies take them on because they expect the borrowed resources to create value.

## Shareholders' Equity

Rearranging the accounting equation:

$$
\text{Equity} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities}
$$

Equity is what would be left for shareholders if the company sold its assets at their book values and paid off its liabilities.[^sec-guide] In Indian financial statements it is often reported as share capital plus **reserves and surplus**: reserves are amounts earmarked for specific purposes, and the surplus holds accumulated profits.

## Retained Earnings

Retained earnings are the part of net income kept in the business instead of being paid out as dividends. They are updated each period:

1. Start with the **beginning retained earnings** carried over from the previous period.
2. **Add net income** (or subtract a net loss).
3. **Subtract dividends** paid to shareholders.
4. The result is the **ending retained earnings**.

$$
\text{Ending RE} = \text{Beginning RE} + \text{Net income} - \text{Dividends}
$$

Retained earnings sit in shareholders' equity and show how much of its growth a company has funded internally.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** beginning retained earnings of 200, net income of 80 and dividends of 30. Before these changes, assets are 1,000, liabilities 600 and equity 400.

**Step 1: ending retained earnings.**

$$
200 + 80 - 30 = 250
$$

**Step 2: check the equation before the period's changes.**

$$
1{,}000 = 600 + 400
$$

Retained earnings rise by 50, so equity rises by 50 as well. The matching 50 appears on the asset side, for example as extra cash, which keeps the balance sheet balanced.
