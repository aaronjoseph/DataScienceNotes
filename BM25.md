# BM25

#search-eng

## Overview

BM25 is a lexical ranking function. It combines term rarity, diminishing returns from repeated occurrences, and document-length normalisation. Start with [[Inverted Index]] and [[TF-IDF]]. [^1]

## A Common Teaching Formula

For query terms $t$, term count $f(t,d)$, document length $|d|$, and average length $avgdl$:

$$score(q,d)=\sum_{t\in q} IDF(t)\frac{f(t,d)(k_1+1)}{f(t,d)+k_1(1-b+b|d|/avgdl)}$$

$k_1>0$ controls term-frequency saturation; $b\in[0,1]$ controls length normalisation. At $b=0$, length normalisation is disabled. [^1]

One nonnegative IDF variant is:

$$IDF(t)=\ln\left(1+\frac{N-df(t)+0.5}{df(t)+0.5}\right)$$

This variant is documented by Lucene. Engine versions can differ in constants, field statistics, and length encoding; the teaching formula is not a promise of byte-for-byte engine scores. [^2]

## Worked Example

Let $N=100$, $df=10$, $f=2$, $|d|=avgdl$, $k_1=1.2$, and $b=0.75$. Then $IDF\approx2.2637$, the frequency factor is $4.4/3.2=1.375$, and this term contributes about $3.1126$.

Increasing $f$ from 2 to 4 does not double the factor: it becomes $8.8/5.2\approx1.6923$. This is saturation.

## Read the Formula as Three Decisions

For one term, IDF expresses collection rarity. The fraction expresses how much evidence repeated occurrences provide, adjusted for document length. Summing term contributions combines this evidence across the analysed query. Here the sum is over distinct query terms; repeated-query-term handling varies by implementation.

Write $A_d=k_1(1-b+b|d|/avgdl)$. The TF factor is $(k_1+1)f/(f+A_d)$. With fixed document length, it approaches $k_1+1$ as $f$ grows. Extra occurrences therefore have diminishing benefit. When $f=0$, the contribution is zero: parameter tuning cannot recover a document with no analysed matching terms.

> [!example]- Solve the longer-document exercise
> With the worked example's parameters but $|d|=2avgdl$, the length term becomes $1.2(0.25+1.5)=2.1$. The TF factor is $4.4/(2+2.1)\approx1.0732$, giving a term score of about 2.4294 instead of 3.1126.
>
> At $b=0$, the length term returns to 1.2 for both documents, so the two scores tie. At $b=1$, the longer document receives the full length adjustment. Which setting is preferable depends on whether longer fields naturally contain more repetitions or genuinely useful additional evidence.

## A Practical Diagnosis Order

For a surprising score, inspect tokenisation, the indexed field, term frequency, field length, and DF before changing $k_1$ or $b$. Compare the engine's explanation output on the same index snapshot. Titles and descriptions have different length distributions, so a sensible configuration for one field need not transfer to another. Tune on held-out judgments and examine query slices such as identifiers and paraphrases.

## Pitfalls and Practice

BM25 scores are not relevance probabilities. They depend on the query, corpus statistics, field analysis, and configuration. A lexical miss may need better analysis or complementary [[Embeddings|semantic retrieval]], not a parameter change.

Recompute the example with a document twice the average length. Then explain how changing $b$ affects the penalty.
