# BM25

#search-eng

## Overview

BM25 is a lexical ranking function. It combines term rarity, diminishing returns from repeated occurrences, and document-length normalisation. Start with [[Inverted Index]] and [[TF-IDF]]. [^1]

## A Common Teaching Formula

Use the following notation:

- $t$: a query term.
- $f(t,d)$: the count of term $t$ in document $d$.
- $|d|$: the document length.
- $avgdl$: the average document length.

The score is

$$
score(q,d)=\sum_{t\in q} IDF(t)\frac{f(t,d)(k_1+1)}{f(t,d)+k_1(1-b+b|d|/avgdl)}
$$

- $k_1>0$ controls term-frequency saturation.
- $b\in[0,1]$ controls length normalisation. At $b=0$, length normalisation is disabled. [^1]

One nonnegative IDF variant is:

$$
IDF(t)=\ln\left(1+\frac{N-df(t)+0.5}{df(t)+0.5}\right)
$$

This variant is documented by Lucene. Engine versions can differ in constants, field statistics, and length encoding; the teaching formula is not a promise of byte-for-byte engine scores. [^2]

## Worked Example

**Inputs**

- Collection size: $N=100$ documents.
- Documents containing the term: $df=10$.
- Term count in this document: $f=2$.
- Document length: $|d|=avgdl$.
- Saturation parameter: $k_1=1.2$.
- Length-normalisation parameter: $b=0.75$.

**1. Calculate IDF.**

$$
IDF=\ln\left(1+\frac{100-10+0.5}{10+0.5}\right)\approx2.2637
$$

**2. Calculate the frequency factor.**

The document has average length, so its length term is $1.2$.

$$
\text{TF factor}=\frac{2(1.2+1)}{2+1.2}=\frac{4.4}{3.2}=1.375
$$

**3. Calculate this term's contribution.**

Multiply IDF by the frequency factor, retaining unrounded values until the final result:

$$
\text{term score}=IDF\times1.375\approx3.1126
$$

**4. Increase the term count to see saturation.**

Keep the document length and other parameters fixed, but increase $f$ from 2 to 4:

$$
\text{TF factor}=\frac{4(1.2+1)}{4+1.2}=\frac{8.8}{5.2}\approx1.6923
$$

The term count doubles, but the factor rises only from **1.375** to **1.6923**. This is saturation.

## Read the Formula as Three Decisions

For one term, IDF expresses collection rarity. The fraction expresses how much evidence repeated occurrences provide, adjusted for document length. Summing term contributions combines this evidence across the analysed query. Here the sum is over distinct query terms; repeated-query-term handling varies by implementation.

Write the length term as

$$
A_d=k_1\left(1-b+b\frac{|d|}{avgdl}\right).
$$

The TF factor becomes

$$
\frac{(k_1+1)f}{f+A_d}.
$$

With fixed document length, this factor approaches $k_1+1$ as $f$ grows. Extra occurrences therefore have diminishing benefit.

When $f=0$, the contribution is zero: parameter tuning cannot recover a document with no analysed matching terms.

> [!example]- Solve the longer-document exercise
> Keep the worked example's parameters, but double the document length: $|d|=2avgdl$.
>
> **1. Calculate the length term.**
>
> $$
> A_d=1.2(0.25+0.75\times2)=2.1
> $$
>
> **2. Calculate the term-frequency factor.**
>
> $$
> \frac{4.4}{2+2.1}\approx1.0732
> $$
>
> **3. Multiply by IDF.**
>
> Using the unrounded values:
>
> $$
> \text{term score}=\ln\left(1+\frac{90.5}{10.5}\right)\times\frac{4.4}{4.1}\approx2.4294
> $$
>
> The score falls from **3.1126** to **2.4294** because the same term count occurs in a longer document.
>
> **How does $b$ change the result?**
>
> - **At $b=0$:** the length term is 1.2 for both documents, so their scores tie.
> - **At $b=1$:** the longer document receives the full length adjustment.
>
> Choose $b$ based on whether longer fields naturally contain more repetitions or provide genuinely useful additional evidence.

## A Practical Diagnosis Order

For a surprising score, inspect tokenisation, the indexed field, term frequency, field length, and DF before changing $k_1$ or $b$.

Compare the engine's explanation output on the same index snapshot.

Titles and descriptions have different length distributions, so a sensible configuration for one field need not transfer to another.

Tune on held-out judgments and examine query slices such as identifiers and paraphrases.

## Pitfalls and Practice

BM25 scores are not relevance probabilities. They depend on the query, corpus statistics, field analysis, and configuration. A lexical miss may need better analysis or complementary [[Embeddings|semantic retrieval]], not a parameter change.

Recompute the example with a document twice the average length. Then explain how changing $b$ affects the penalty.
